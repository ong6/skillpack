## 1 · Decision-dimension checklist (the gap-analysis menu)

In Step 2, walk this list and mark each dimension **known / unknown / N-A** for the
current request. Raise the *unknowns that would change the recommendation*. Most of
these are blind spots buyers omit — that's exactly what to clarify.

| # | Dimension | Why it changes the recommendation | Example clarifying question |
|---|-----------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------|
| 1 | **Budget — range + hard/soft** | Sets the contender set; "flexible" vs "ceiling" leads to different picks | "Is your budget a hard ceiling, or would you stretch ~20% for a clearly better pick?" |
| 2 | **Total cost of ownership** | Consumables, subscriptions, accessories, energy can dwarf sticker price | "Are you accounting for ongoing costs (refills / subscriptions / accessories), or just upfront?" |
| 3 | **Primary job-to-be-done** | The actual task, not the category, decides fit | "What's the single most important thing it has to do well?" |
| 4 | **Usage intensity & frequency** | Occasional vs daily vs pro changes the durability/spec tier | "Daily heavy use, or occasional?" |
| 5 | **Users & skill level** | Beginner-friendly vs power-user features; shared use | "Who uses it — just you, or others? Beginner or experienced?" |
| 6 | **Environment / constraints** | Space, climate, noise, power, portability can be knockouts | "Any space, noise, or portability limits I should respect?" |
| 7 | **Ecosystem / compatibility** | Existing devices, platform, accessories, standards lock options in/out | "Does it need to work with anything you already own (platform / accessories / standard)?" |
| 8 | **Region & availability** | Models, prices, warranty, and support vary by country | "Which country are you buying in, and do you need local warranty/support?" |
| 9 | **Timeline** | Buy-now vs wait-for-refresh-or-sale changes the answer | "Buying now, or can you wait for a new model / sale?" |
| 10 | **Priorities & trade-offs (ranked)** | The tie-breaker — rank price, performance, durability, design, features, support, resale | "If you had to rank price / performance / durability / design, what's the order?" |
| 11 | **Dealbreakers / must-haves** | Hard filters that eliminate otherwise-great options | "Anything that's an absolute must-have or an instant no?" |
| 12 | **Longevity & upgrade path** | How long they keep it; repairability; resale | "How long do you want this to last before replacing?" |
| 13 | **Risk tolerance** | Cutting-edge vs proven; new vs refurbished/used | "Prefer the latest, or proven-and-reliable? Open to refurbished?" |
| 14 | **Already considered / rejected** | Avoid re-recommending what they've dismissed | "Anything you've already looked at and ruled out — and why?" |

**Selection rule:** only ask about a gap when its answer would *flip or reorder* the
shortlist, then keep just the top 2–4 and ask them as one round of tap-to-answer MCQs.
Three sharp multiple-choice questions beat a dozen generic ones.

## 2 · Category blind spots (common "forgot to mention" factors)

Quick prompts for the factor buyers most often omit. Not exhaustive — adapt.

- **Laptops / phones / electronics:** ecosystem lock-in, repairability, battery life under *real* load, port selection, software-support lifespan.
- **Large appliances:** dimensions + doorway/clearance, energy cost, noise level, installation/plumbing/voltage, service network.
- **Mattresses / furniture:** firmness vs sleep position/body weight, return/trial window, off-gassing, delivery & removal.
- **Cars / e-bikes:** total cost (insurance, fuel/charging, service), range/MPG in real conditions, resale, parts availability.
- **Software / SaaS:** seat/usage pricing at scale, data export & lock-in, integration with existing stack, support SLA.
- **Cameras / hobby gear:** the *system* (lenses/accessories) not just the body, learning curve, weight for actual use.
- **Baby / safety gear:** certifications/standards, recall history, fit with car/home, longevity across ages.
- **Tools / appliances with consumables:** battery platform compatibility, consumable cost & availability over time.

## 3 · Source-quality rubric (Step 4)

Weight evidence roughly in this order:

1. **Independent hands-on / lab testing** (measured results, repeatable methodology) — highest.
2. **Aggregated owner reviews at scale** — best for reliability & failure modes; read the *1–3 star* reviews for real complaints.
3. **Domain community / forums** (enthusiast subreddits, specialist forums) — surfaces long-term issues and quirks.
4. **Manufacturer spec sheets** — facts about specs, but marketing on quality/claims; verify independently.
5. **Affiliate "best-of" listicles & sponsored content** — lowest; useful for discovering contenders, not for ranking them.

**Red flags:** all-positive review profiles, reviews clustered on one date (astroturf),
"#1 best" with affiliate links and no methodology, specs quoted without real-world
testing, recommendations that ignore the buyer's stated constraints.

**Always:** confirm the model is *current* (not a discontinued/older revision), check
whether a refresh is imminent, and get a current price/availability read for the
buyer's region before recommending.

### Technical products: evaluate the substance, not the marketing

Assume the open web is mostly marketing. For products where performance is objective
or governed by science/engineering, don't stop at review sentiment — go to the
evidence that actually predicts performance.

**When this matters:** electronics (CPUs/GPUs, displays, storage, networking), optics
& cameras, audio, batteries/power, water & air filtration, skincare/sunscreen actives,
supplements & health claims, materials & tools, anything sold on a number.

**How to evaluate:**
1. **Find the 1–3 specs/mechanisms that truly drive performance** for *the buyer's job* — not the headline feature. (A monitor's panel type + measured response + gamut coverage matters more than "gaming-grade.")
2. **Get objective evidence:** independent lab measurements & benchmarks, recognized **standards & certifications**, teardowns, manufacturer **datasheets/whitepapers** (for raw specs, not quality claims), and peer-reviewed studies or genuine domain experts.
3. **Translate every claim into its measurable backing** — "what number or test proves this?" No number → treat it as marketing.
4. **Separate meaningful specs from vanity metrics** and proprietary made-up units.

**Technical-marketing red flags:** proprietary metrics with no standard behind them;
"up to X" peak/best-case figures; "clinically proven" / "lab tested" with no citation,
dose, or sample size; spec sheets that omit the spec that matters; comparisons against
unnamed "leading brands"; self-awarded "certifications."

**Marketing term → what actually determines fit (go find this):**

| Category | Marketing pushes | The substance to verify |
|----------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Monitor | "gaming", "HDR-ready", "1ms" | panel type (IPS/VA/OLED), *measured* response/overshoot, gamut % (sRGB/DCI-P3), real HDR tier (DisplayHDR 600+) |
| Sunscreen | "SPF 100", "natural" | broad-spectrum UVA filters used, PPD/PA rating, photostability |
| Water filter | "purifies", "advanced filtration" | NSF/ANSI cert # + *which* contaminants, flow rate, filter life/cost |
| Headphones | "studio", "deep bass" | measured frequency response & distortion, driver type, isolation |
| Supplement | "boosts immunity", "high potency" | actual dose vs studied dose, bioavailable form, independent purity testing |
| Power bank | "fast", "massive capacity" | real Wh + rated output (W), measured efficiency, protocols (PD/PPS) |

When you recommend, state the *evidence basis* ("measured by <independent lab>",
"NSF-certified for X"), and flag any claim you could only find on marketing pages.

## 5 · Worked example (compressed)

**Buyer (Step 1):** "Want a robot vacuum, under $400, for a small apartment with a cat.
Looked at Roomba but heard they're loud."

**Gap analysis (Step 2) flags as material unknowns:** flooring type (#3/#6), mopping
needed? (#3), self-empty dock vs budget (#2/#10), app/ecosystem prefs (#7), pet-hair
tangle (a #4/durability concern), noise threshold (#6, they raised it).

**Clarify (Step 3, via AskUserQuestion):**
- Mostly hard floors, carpet, or mixed? *(decides brush type & suction)*
- Need mopping too, or vacuum only? *(halves the field)*
- Self-emptying dock worth eating into the $400, or spend it all on the robot? *(TCO vs upfront)*
- How important is quiet, 1–5? *(you flagged Roomba noise)*

**Research (Step 4):** triangulate hands-on tests + owner reviews focused on *cat-hair
tangling and dock reliability* + a robot-vacuum community for long-term quirks; keep to
3 contenders under $400 in the buyer's region; check each is the current model.

**Deliver (Step 5):** top pick + one runner-up ("quieter but no mop"), a 3-row trade-off
table on quietness / pet-hair handling / mopping / price, the common regret (cheap models
that tangle on long hair), price/availability check, and confidence + "would change if
you actually have more carpet than you think."

## 6 · Track B — pricing reference (Find the best price)

Depth for `SKILL.md`'s Track B. Use when sweeping listings, judging a deal, and ranking by
true cost.

### 6.1 · Landed cost — rank by what you actually pay

```
landed cost = sticker
            + shipping
            + import duty / GST / sales tax (if it crosses a border or isn't tax-in)
            - currently-valid coupon / cashback / card discount
```

Compare **landed cost in one currency**, never sticker vs. sticker across regions. The
cache won't convert for you — it flags mixed currencies; you do the FX at *today's* rate
and say which rate/date you used. A cheaper sticker abroad often loses once duty + shipping
land.

### 6.2 · Fake-discount tells (a "deal" that isn't)

- **Inflated "was" price** — the strike-through RRP was never really charged. Check the
  *typical* price on a tracker (§6.4), not the seller's own anchor.
- **Permanent "sale"** — the price has sat at the "discounted" level for months (Keepa/CCC
  history shows a flat line, not a dip).
- **Manufactured urgency** — "2 left!", countdown timers, "deal ends in 1h" that resets.
- **Bundle padding** — "$200 of free accessories" that are low-value or unwanted.
- **Coupon theatre** — a code that only reverses an inflated base price.
- **Model-year swap** — last year's model discounted *next to* the current one, presented as
  the same thing.

A real deal beats the **recent street price** (the actual trend line), not the RRP.

### 6.3 · Grey-market / counterfeit red flags

Price far below every legitimate seller is a warning, not a win:

- Seller is a no-name third-party marketplace account with thin/young feedback.
- "No manufacturer warranty" / "international version" / "imported" on a domestic listing.
- Stock photos only, mismatched box/region, missing serial or tamper-seal mentions.
- Payment pushed off-platform, or wire/crypto-only.
- For software/keys: OEM/volume keys sold as retail, "lifetime" subscriptions — likely
  leaked or fraudulent. Stay on official refurb/used marketplaces with buyer protection.

### 6.4 · Region → trusted tracker / aggregator

Use the right tracker for the buyer's market; many expose APIs (prefer those — §SKILL Track B step 3).

| Region | Aggregators (current price) | History / "good time to buy?" |
|--------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| US | Google Shopping, PriceGrabber | **Keepa**, **camelcamelcamel** (Amazon) |
| UK | Google Shopping, PriceRunner, **PriceSpy** | Keepa, CamelCamelCamel |
| EU (DE/wide) | **Idealo**, Geizhals (DE/AT) | Idealo price history, Keepa |
| Nordics | **PriceSpy / Prisjakt**, PriceRunner | PriceSpy history |
| SG / SEA | Shopee, Lazada, **PriceSpy SG**, brand stores | Keepa (Amazon.sg), seller history |
| AU | Google Shopping, StaticICE | Keepa (Amazon.au), CamelCamelCamel |

Category trackers (GPUs, flights, etc.) often beat general ones — use them when they exist.

### 6.5 · Deal verdict — "good now / wait / average"

- **Good now** — at or below the recent street low, and no scheduled sale event imminent.
- **Wait** — a known sale window is close (Black Friday, Prime Day, EOFY/AU, 6.6/9.9/11.11
  in SEA, back-to-school), or a model refresh is near (this year's price drops when next
  year's lands).
- **Average** — mid-range vs. history; buy if you need it now, hold if you don't.

Always state the **recent low** for context and the **date** you checked.
